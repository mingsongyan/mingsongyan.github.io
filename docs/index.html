About

I am a Visiting Assistant Professor in the Department of Mathematics at the University of California, Santa Barbara, where I work with Prof. Sui Tang. At UCSB, I also co-mentor undergraduate and graduate researchers with Prof. Tang and organize the Applied Math/PDE/Data Science Seminar.

I received my Ph.D. in Computational and Applied Mathematics from Old Dominion University in 2024 under the supervision of Prof. Yuesheng Xu. My dissertation was titled Deep Learning in Reproducing Kernel Banach Spaces. Previously, I earned my B.S. through the Tang Aoqing Honors Program in Mathematics at Jilin University in China in 2018.

Research

My research focuses on the mathematical foundations of efficient and interpretable machine learning. Using tools from optimization, functional analysis, and dynamical systems, I study how mathematical structure can help us understand and improve modern learning models, particularly neural networks and large language models.

My work centers on two closely connected themes:

Efficiency

  • Sparse optimization: Developing sparse regularization methods and algorithms for signal and image processing and deep learning, aiming to improve computational efficiency and reconstruction or prediction accuracy.
  • Neural ODEs: Understanding when Neural ODEs trained on small graphs can transfer to larger, structurally similar graphs, reducing the need for expensive training on large graphs.
  • Low-rank structure in LLMs: Exploring low-rank structure in LLM activations to guide architecture design and improve pretraining efficiency.

Interpretability

  • In-context learning: Studying how transformers learn from examples in context, with a focus on their connections to kernel ridge regression and iterative algorithms.
  • Function spaces: Developing function-space frameworks for deep learning, including hypothesis spaces and representer theorems.
